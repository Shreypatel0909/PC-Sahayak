# Overheating and Thermal Problems

## Overview
A PC that runs hot will slow itself down (thermal throttling), shut off unexpectedly, or become unstable to protect its components. Overheating is usually caused by blocked airflow, dust buildup, failing fans, or dried-out thermal paste. Most fixes are simple cleaning and placement changes; the invasive ones are a last resort.

## Common symptoms
- Fans run loud and fast most of the time.
- The system slows down under load, then recovers when idle.
- Sudden shutdowns or restarts, especially during gaming or heavy tasks.
- The case, keyboard, or underside of a laptop feels very hot.
- Temperature readings above roughly 90-95 degrees Celsius under load.

## Diagnostic questions
- When does it get hot: idle, light use, or only heavy tasks?
- Is it a laptop or a desktop, and where is it placed?
- When was it last cleaned of dust?
- Are all fans spinning, and do you hear any unusual noise?
- Has it been overclocked or recently moved?

## Step-by-step fixes

### Check the environment and airflow
1. Place the PC on a hard, flat surface. For laptops, avoid beds, couches, and laps that block the vents underneath.
2. Make sure intake and exhaust vents are not against a wall or inside an enclosed cabinet. Leave several centimeters of clearance.
3. Monitor temperatures with a reputable hardware-monitor utility to confirm the problem and see which component is hot (CPU or GPU).

### Reduce the load
4. Open **Task Manager** and close or end processes using high CPU or GPU that you do not need.
5. In **Settings > System > Power**, choose a more balanced power mode to lower heat output, especially on laptops.

### Clean the dust
> **Warning:** Power off and unplug the PC first. For laptops, also disconnect the charger. Use short bursts of compressed air and hold fans still so they do not spin and generate voltage. Do not open a device still under warranty if opening voids it.

6. Blow dust out of the vents, fans, and heatsink fins with compressed air. Dust buildup is the most common cause of gradual overheating.
7. Confirm every fan spins freely and none are obstructed by cables or debris.

### Verify cooling hardware
8. Check that case fans and the CPU/GPU cooler are firmly seated and their cables are connected. A disconnected fan header will leave a component uncooled.
9. In firmware (BIOS/UEFI) or a vendor utility, confirm fan curves are not set to silent/minimum when the system is under load.

### Advanced: thermal paste and overclocks
> **Warning:** Reapplying thermal paste and reseating a cooler require opening the device and can damage components if done incorrectly. Attempt only if you are comfortable, and discharge static first. Reset any overclock to stock before blaming hardware.

10. If a system several years old still overheats after cleaning, the thermal paste between the CPU/GPU and its cooler may have dried out and can be reapplied.

## When to seek professional help
- Temperatures stay dangerously high after cleaning and checking fans.
- A fan is failing (grinding, rattling, or not spinning) and needs replacement.
- The device shuts off within minutes of starting, which risks damage; stop using it until inspected.
